Campaign Management 101 Campaign Management Fundamentals

Course intro

As we’ve learned in the course "Introduction to Campaign Management", our system is vital in helping you optimize and manage your campaigns throughout the entire campaign lifecycle. Campaign Management was specifically built to simplify operations, solve for data inconsistency and enforce global best practice governance. 

Campaign Management enables the following advantages for users who juggle many different platforms and inventory across global markets:

1
Operational workflow management (via campaign setup workflow, standardization of naming convention, notifications on spends, etc.)

2
Financial Management, Risk Mitigation & Compliance (via budget management in Campaign Management, monthly spend confirmation & reconciliation, supplier invoice approval, client approval, etc.)

3
Ongoing reporting & advanced analytics (via trafficking workflow, mapping to advertising platforms, data management, etc.)

In this course, we'll focus on the Operational workflow management in Campaign Management

After agency teams have completed planning, Campaign Management acts as the organizational backbone of the media campaign - from media plan approval, buy execution, and reconciliation for digital spend as well as offline budgets -, providing a holistic view of the media in one easy to use web application.

You should already be familiar with the structure of Campaign Management, so let's dive right in and explore the first step to launching a media campaign - creating a Campaign Management campaign!

Creating Media Plan Lines
Lesson 4 of 8
A media plan moves through various stages during the campaign lifecycle. Before we move on, let's take a look at the progress bar that you find on top of a media plan.

During its lifetime, a media plan moves through eight (8) stages. As the media plan progresses, each new stage that has been reached will turn green. You can always switch to view the media plan in a previous stage via the toggle below the progress bar, e.g. to create placements or to make edits to plan lines.

Draft
In this mode, users can make changes to their media buys (edia plan lines) and add new plan lines.

Published
All changes have been committed and information will be used by the Finance team for forecasting & reporting purposes.



Tasks that typically happen in the published view of a media plan:

Create Olive placements
Link Olive IOs to client POs
Upload countersigned supplier IOs after the media plan has been client approved
(You'll learn about those tasks later in this training program.)

Internal Approval Requested
Media teams requests internal review of the final media plan from the agency client/campaign lead.

Internally Approved
The agency client/campaign lead has approved the media plan before campaigns go live and the client’s money is spent.

Client Approval Requested
The agency client/campaign lead has requested approval of the media plan from the client.

Client Approved
The client has approved the media plan and authorized to start spending their money on those media buys.

Reconciliation
The media plan has ended and automatically moves into this stage. Media teams are responsible to confirm their final spends on all media buys (plan lines).

Reconciled
The finance team has locked the plan. No more changes can be made.

Now that we’ve reviewed how to create a Campaign Management and media plan, we need to understand what role plan lines play within each campaign. 

There are TWO types of plan lines you can create in Campaign Management.

Media plan lines

Media plan lines resemble the individual biddable and non-biddable as well as offline media buys of where we’ll spend the budget. 

They are typically created in Campaign Management by the Activation or Tactical Planning teams.

Service plan lines
Service plan lines refer to components of the media plan that are not media but instead additional charges to the client such as agency commission, tech fee, production fee. 

Some types of service plan lines are created in Campaign Management  by agency Planning teams (e.g. agency comms), and other types are created by the Activation team (e.g. platform fees).

Creating Plan Lines
When creating plan lines, you can choose between the following three (3) options:

Creating One Plan Line
To create one plan line, you need to:

1) Navigate to your media plan and select Draft View.


2) Click on Add Line.


3) In the new window that opens, toggle Cost type to Media.


4) Complete the fields. An overview of the dependencies between Media type, Channel, Buy type, and Platform fields can be found in this document:
 

Section: Free-form field that can be used to structure the media plan.
Name: Free-form field to provide a short summary of the plan line/buy.
Region: The location in which users are targeted and served ads to.
Goal: One goal per plan line. Select based on the goals set on media plan level.
Channel: The sector of advertising that the media is for (e.g. search, social, display).
Device: The devices that the ad will be targeting. Select Hybrid unless there’s a reason you’re not targeting users on all available devices.
Media type: The format of the ad.
Buy type: How the media space will be purchased (e.g. direct, programmatically).
Platform: The self-service platform that the media is running through. If direct buy, select Other. Selecting the wrong platform causes issues with financial reconciliation. Once the media plan is client approved, this field cannot be changed. Users will need to reach out to the Campaign Management Support team and follow instructions outlined in this knowledge base article.
Property: The site on which media is running on.
Liable entity: The entity responsible to pay the supplier for the media (agency vs. client). Selecting the wrong entity causes issues with financial reconciliation. Once the media plan is client approved, this field cannot be changed. Users will need to reach out to the Campaign Management Support team and follow instructions outlined in this knowledge base article.
Supplier: The owner who is providing the advertising space. This document provides a list of the correct suppliers to use for popular platforms globally. Selecting the wrong supplier causes issues with data integrity and financial reconciliation. Once the media plan is client approved, this field cannot be changed. Users will need to reach out to the Campaign Management Support team and follow instructions outlined in this knowledge base article. 
Please take extra care to ensure you're adding the correct supplier to your plan line. Please check in this guide for further instructions on how to set up suppliers across EMEA MFG Campaign Management media plans.
Cost model: The pricing structure of the media (fixed vs. biddable/variable). This document provides an overview of the reporting data source depending on the selected cost model. For information on added value buys, please refer to this article.
Start and End dates: Flight dates of the media for this plan line.
Budget currency: The currency in which the media is being bought in. Selecting the wrong currency causes issues with financial reconciliation. Once the media plan is client approved, this field cannot be changed. Users will need to reach out to the Campaign Management Support team and follow instructions outlined in this knowledge base article.
Budget net: The amount you expect to be invoice for the media, with discounts already applied. Please ensure to always keep your budget up to date prior to the end of the flight.
Distribute budget: Click this button to evenly distribute the budget across the months of your plan line flight. You can manually override the values and modify the monthly budgets. Find out more in this demo.
Latent Conversion End Date: The last day to record conversions after the media flight (e.g. 30 days after the end date for a 30-day post-click conversion window).
Advertising stream: Stream 1 is for standard advertising, stream 2 is for custom execution.
Description: A short note about this plan line.
Placement label: A label used to differentiate placements that share the same property and region with placements from another plan line. This label will be added to all placements for this plan line.
5) Click on Create.

If you would like to have additional custom fields added to the plan line form for your client, please reach out to the Choreograph team.

Editing Plan Lines
When editing plan lines, you can choose between the following two (2) options:
 

editing one plan line at a time
editing multiple plan lines in bulk
Editing One Plan Line
To edit one plan line, you need to:

1) Navigate to your media plan and select Draft View.

2) Click on the pencil button to the right of the plan line you want to edit. The plan line view opens.


3) Make changes to your plan line. Changes will be highlighted in the media plan view.

4) Click Save if you wish to make changes to another plan line or click Save And Publish if you're done making changes to your media plan.

5) Click Publish All Changes when you're done making additional changes to your media plan.

Bulk Editing Plan Lines
Instead of editing plan lines one at a time, you may choose to make changes to multiple plan lines in bulk. This functionality is currently only available for media plan lines that have a budget set.

To edit plan lines in bulk:

1) Navigate to your media plan and select Draft View.

2) Select the media plan lines you want to edit via the tick boxes to the left of the plan lines. An Actions button will appear on top.

3) Click on the Actions button and select Bulk edit media plan lines from the dropdown. 


A spreadsheet view will appear with one plan line populated per row, and the five (5) most commonly changed fields as columns.

4) Use the toggle Show all fields on the top left to unhide all remaining plan line fields for editing.

5) Make changes to your plan line fields. Note that you won't be able to change the first column for plan line ID as this is an automatically generated field.

6) Fix any issues that may be flagged by Campaign Management due to incorrect field entries.


7) Click Submit. This will take you back to the media plan view where you will see a success message that plan lines have been edited.

8) Click Publish All Changes.

Campaign Management FAQs

What if your media buy is a freebie?

In Campaign Management, you can easily indicate that a line on your media plan is a freebie ("Added Value") and still track impressions and clicks to include in reports. You should select "Added Value" as your plan line cost model, which means that Campaign Management automatically sets the budget to zero. 

You can read up about this in more detail on the Campaign Management knowledge base(opens in a new tab).

How can I change the supplier/liable entity/currency/platform on my plan line if I selected the wrong one?
Once a media plan has been approved by the client and reached the Client Approved stage, or is in Reconciliation/Reconciled stage, users can no longer change the supplier, liable entity, currency or platform fields on their plan line. 

If your media plan is in the Client Approved or Reconciliation stage, you may reach out to the Campaign Management Support team for help. To fix the issue:
 

If monthly spend has already been confirmed for your media plan, update confirmed spends to 0.01 for one month and 0 for the rest of the months.
Submit a ticket to the Campaign Management Support team, asking them to push the media plan back to its Published stage.
Downweight the plan line with incorrect supplier by setting its budget to 0.01. This will cancel the existing incorrect Campaign Management IO that the plan line belongs to.
Create a new plan line with the correct supplier and assign the correct budget.
Publish the media plan.
Submit a ticket to the Campaign Management Support team, asking them to move placements from the old to the new plan line, if required.
Request internal and client approval for your media plan.
If the media plan has already been locked by Client Finance and moved into the Reconciled stage, please follow these steps:
 

Email our agency Client Finance team, asking them to un-reconcile the media plan.
If monthly spend has already been confirmed for your media plan, update confirmed spends to 0.01 for one month and 0 for the rest of the months.
Submit a ticket to the Campaign Management Support team, asking them to push the media plan back to its Published stage.
Downweight the plan line with incorrect supplier by setting its budget to 0.01. This will cancel the existing incorrect Campaign Management IO that the plan line belongs to.
Create a new plan line with the correct supplier and assign the correct budget.
Publish the media plan.
Submit a ticket to the Campaign Management Support team, asking them to move placements from the old to the new plan line, if required.
Request internal and client approval for your media plan.
Email our agency Client Finance team, asking them to re-reconcile the media plan.
What type of fee should I choose for DV360 platform fees?
You should choose the Management Tools plan line type for that. Do not add this to your media spend as you would then have incorrect agency fees.

Are fees client liable or agency liable?
Our agency is typically liable for fees. More information on how to set up service plan lines in Campaign Management can be found in this knowledge base article.

How can I add offline suppliers and properties to Campaign Management?
For new suppliers for Google India/Korea/Germany/China/Korea or any client who plans offline campaigns, a ticket should be raised with the Campaign Management Support team. They will be able to directly add suppliers into Campaign Management in bulk.

To add a new property to Campaign Management, a ticket should be raised with our agency AdOps team.

Is there a "read only" option to access Campaign Management?
Yes! Users may request the Read Only role from the EMC and GroupM Finance teams.

This role allows you to access the common areas of Campaign Management, such as campaigns, media plans, plan lines, exchange rates, and view the data, but you won't be able to edit, create or delete it.

This Read Only role may be particularly handy for auditors or senior stakeholders for monitoring purpose.

Why is this important, you ask?

1
Internal media plan approval

2
Client media plan approval

The client you have requested approval from will now receive an email from Campaign Management about the approval request. If you have selected multiple approvers, ALL of them need to approve the media plan in order for the plan to be client approved. To delete an approver, simply click on the bin icon next to the approver's name.

Note: Some teams do not ask their client to approve media plans in Campaign Management directly as this is handled via other means. However, for SOX compliance and financial audit reasons, client media plan approval is required to be provided in Campaign Management. Therefore, those client teams typically appoint a "proxy" approver at the agency within the CS team who will approve the media plan on behalf of the client.


Great job, your plan is now client approved. What's next?

After the media plan has been client approved, Activation teams are typically responsible to upload countersigned supplier IOs for their media buys. You'll learn more about this in the Campaign Management 301 training course.

What happens if I need to make changes to the media plan after approval?

Once a media plan has been approved by the client, you can of course still make changes - add or remove suppliers, change flight dates, shift around budget etc. All those changes need to be made in the Draft view of the media plan (use the toggle below the progress bar to hop between plan stages). Simply edit your plan lines and publish the changes. 

Some changes to the media plan may trigger a breach of client approval and require the client to re-approve the media plan. Without the re-approval, there is a risk of running unapproved campaigns.

Now What?
To avoid sending multiple notifications and re-approval requests to your client when making changes that may breach the Media Plan, users are advised to make all changes before publishing the Media Plan instead of publishing individual changes one-by-one. 

In any of the scenarios listed in the table above, published changes to a media plan will trigger a notification to the media plan manager, and most require internal re-approval of affected Campaign Management IOs. 

If a change is going to breach the client-approved media plan, users will see a notification in Campaign Management to confirm whether they want to go ahead and publish the change to breach the media plan.


The next steps for users are:

1) Click on Continue & Publish to move the media plan into the Breached stage.

2) Request internal media plan approval. The Internal Approver will be able to approve all changed IOs with one click or approve each IO individually.

3) Request media plan re-approval from client(s) if the media plan was breached. If the media plan wasn’t breached, it will move automatically back into the Client Approved stage once internal approval has been given.
